| **`.xlsb` workbooks are found by all their cells, old `.xls` and `.xlsb` are read with a fraction of the memory, and long ones are found by what their last sheets say.** The cells after the first of a row, which Excel writes in a short form in an `.xlsb`, were left out; they are read now, so **every `.xlsb` is read again, once**, and gets its vectors again. An `.xls` is read record by record from the file, a sheet where it lies, and an `.xlsb` as it is unpacked: a 75 MB `.xls` takes 29 MB to read instead of 162 MB, an `.xlsb` with a 60 MB sheet 28 MB instead of 88 MB. An `.xls` with about 3 MB of text or more gets its passages for search by meaning from the whole workbook: those get their vectors made again, once; no other `.xls` is read again. The desktop app previews `.xlsb` as tables too, and **F3** in the terminal app shows a workbook (`.xlsx`, `.xlsm`, `.xlsb`, `.xls`, `.ods`) as its sheets' text. | [Documents it reads](search/documents.md#are-old-xls-and-binary-xlsb-workbooks-read) | <!-- feature -->
